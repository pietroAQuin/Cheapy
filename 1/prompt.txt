the answer: The frontier plot is mostly an evaluation artifact, not a charting exercise. What makes it credible:

Axes & units
x = cost per trajectory (cache-aware, estimated tokens — state your token estimator), y = your quality proxy. Caption exactly how both are computed.
Use per-trajectory means (or per 1k); don't mix totals and means.

Anchors first
Always-cheapest, always-strongest, the observed policy, and random routing at matched cost. Your curve only means something relative to those four.

Sweep, don't cherry-pick
Generate the curve by sweeping one knob (escalation threshold / budget), not by tuning a new model per point. Keep Pareto-dominant points, show dominated ones faintly so the sweep is visible.

Uncertainty is the whole game
Off-policy estimates need CIs: bootstrap over trajectories, draw bands not bare lines. A frontier without error bands reads as overfit.
Show effective sample size / overlap where weights blow up; mark thin-support regions as unreliable instead of extrapolating through them.

Details judges notice
Held-out split for the headline curve; label train vs held-out.
A sensitivity variant (±30% on cache-hit rate or price assumptions) as a second faint frontier shows your ranking is robust.
Annotate 2–3 named operating points ("60% of strongest-model cost, 97% of its quality") — that's the line people remember.

One clean chart: light grid, direct labels instead of a legend, no dual y-axes.

Both are valid sweeps — they're just different knobs, and which one you can defend depends on your data.

Per-call threshold (no cascade)

You score each trajectory with a "needs the strong model" signal (difficulty proxy, classifier probability, tool presence, length) and route if the score exceeds τ. Sweeping τ from 0→1 traces the frontier: τ=1 is always-cheapest, τ=0 is always-strongest. This is the cheapest to evaluate honestly, because each trajectory gets exactly one model and your off-policy estimate is a straightforward reweighting.

Budget-constrained routing

Same scores, but instead of a threshold you allocate the top-k highest-benefit trajectories to the strong model until a cost cap is hit. Sweeping the cap gives the frontier. Slightly stronger story (it's explicitly an allocation problem — spend where marginal quality per euro is highest), but it's a batch/oracle policy unless you show an online version with a threshold calibrated to hit the budget in expectation.

True cascade

Run cheap first, escalate on a signal from its own output (self-reported confidence, verifier, retry heuristics). Sweeping the escalation threshold gives the frontier. This is the most interesting policy and the hardest to justify on trajectories_v1: there are no outputs and no usage in the data, so you cannot observe what the cheap model would have produced or whether it would have triggered escalation. You'd be simulating a signal you never measured — and you must charge the cascade for both calls, plus the cache reset on the switch, or the numbers are fiction.

Practical read: make (1) or (2) your headline frontier, since the traces actually support them. If you want the cascade, present it as a clearly labeled modeled extension with explicit assumptions (escalation rate, cheap-call cost always paid, cache penalty) and a sensitivity band. Judges reward the honesty far more than the extra sophistication.
Whichever you pick, say in the caption what the knob is and that a single knob generated every point — that's what separates a sweep from cherry-picked runs.
